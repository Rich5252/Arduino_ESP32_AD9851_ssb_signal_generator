Two-tone IMD test — 14175 kHz

Speaker-into-mic two-tone drive, comp on vs comp off, two tone pairs (700/1700Hz and 700/1900Hz), captured 2026-09-29 via SDRuno.

Update: the 9.3 dB asymmetry was mostly a noise-contaminated measurement bin, not the transmitter

Re-running the test with the tone pair pushed up to 700/1900 Hz — which moves the high-side third-order product from +2700 Hz up to +3100 Hz — collapses the asymmetry from ~9.3 dB down to roughly 0.9–2.7 dB. That means the original +2700 Hz reading was largely picking up background noise sitting at that specific frequency in the 250 Hz measurement window, not a genuine third-order product from the transmitter. Moving off that frequency reveals the real figure, and it's a good one: roughly −32 to −35 dBc on both sides, with only a small, unremarkable residual asymmetry — the "asymmetry we've always seen," per your note, but nowhere near the scale the 700/1700 Hz run suggested. The earlier report's attribution of that 9.3 dB gap to the project's known relative_delay_samples-sensitive Low/High 3rd-IMD effect is superseded by this finding — that mechanism may still contribute a dB or two (matching the small residual seen here), but it was not the dominant cause of the original reading.

What this shows

In both tone pairs the two drive tones sit within 0.7 dB of each other (0.02–0.66 dB across the four runs), so neither comb is an artifact of uneven drive — it reflects what the transmitter and measurement together actually produced. On the original 700/1700Hz pair, the closest-in high-side product (2f2−f1, +2700Hz) read −27.2 to −27.6 dBc against a low-side (2f1−f2, −300Hz) of −36.7 dBc — a ~9.3 dB gap that looked like a real, structural transmitter asymmetry. Shifting the pair to 700/1900Hz moves those two products to −500/+3100Hz and the gap nearly disappears: −35.1 to −35.3 dBc low side vs −32.3 to −34.4 dBc high side, a residual 0.9–2.7 dB. Since moving the measurement frequency is the only thing that changed, the simplest explanation is the right one: something (most plausibly a modest, narrowband noise/spur contribution, not a broad noise floor) was elevating readings specifically near +2700Hz, and the 700/1700Hz run's "IMD3" figure was measuring that contribution as much as the transmitter's own third-order product.

The 700/1900Hz re-test's near-in high side isn't perfectly clean either — both +3100Hz (3rd order, 2f2−f1) and +4300Hz (5th order, 3f2−2f1) read within 2 dB of each other (−34.4/−32.7 dBc comp on, −32.3/−34.6 dBc comp off), rather than the 5th-order term dropping cleanly below the 3rd as a simple nonlinearity would suggest. That's a smaller, secondary wrinkle worth another look sometime, not the headline finding here.

Beyond the near-in pair, both combs in both tests roll off in a fairly clean, monotonic ladder spaced at the two-tone difference frequency (1 kHz for the 700/1700Hz pair, 1.2 kHz for 700/1900Hz) — typical spectral regrowth for this EER/polar architecture. The 700/1900Hz pair reaches noticeably further down by its far rungs (−59 to −61 dBc by the 9th/10th product) than the 700/1700Hz pair did (−56 to −58 dBc) — consistent with the absolute levels being "very good," as you put it, once the near-in reading isn't being inflated by whatever sits near 2700Hz.

Comparing comp on against comp off at matched envelope peak, in both tone pairs the two conditions track each other closely — most products differ by well under a dB to a couple of dB, with no consistent sign either way. That still reads as the compressor not meaningfully costing (or buying) linearity at this operating point, in both tests.

Note on the uploaded capture

The uploaded WAV (700/1700Hz run only — no capture was uploaded for the 700/1900Hz re-test) is a raw SDRuno IQ recording (stereo 16-bit, 388.888 kHz sample rate, 4.45 s, centred on 14175 kHz) with a steady, non-transient envelope throughout — consistent with a single steady-state capture of one of the two conditions, not a combined recording of both. A quick independent FFT located the two test tones close to their nominal 700/1700 Hz offsets and confirmed the general two-tone structure, but a naive whole-file transform over the full recording bandwidth also picks up other energy (band activity on a shared 20m allocation, SDR passband-edge artifacts) that a dedicated, targeted measurement tool correctly excludes. This report is built directly from the numbers your own test harness reported for both tone pairs, with the WAV kept as the archived reference capture for the first run rather than as a second quantitative source — and its existence doesn't change the noise-contamination finding above, which comes from comparing the two harness-reported sweeps directly.
